# Construction and evidence

## Evidence ledger

Keep an internal list of observed, continued and designed details. Multiple images resolve uncertainty only when they clearly depict the same outfit. Do not blend different clothes because their colors match. A supplied rear reference overrides earlier rear assumptions. Ask about conflicting defining features.

An unseen back is a plausible proposal, not verified product documentation. A newly created lower garment is designed even if convincing. When the source is too small to identify a garment category, ask for a clearer reference instead of manufacturing certainty.

## Shared geometry

Lock item count, layer order, collar and shoulder construction, sleeve length, waist height, hem length/width and trouser silhouette. Preserve intentional trains or uneven hems only where supported. Do not lengthen the rear for dramatic effect.

Connect front and rear through plausible shoulder and side seams. Continue all-over pattern scale without stamping the whole front onto the rear. Front-only graphics stay on the front. Do not invent duplicate fasteners.

Name asymmetry using garment-relative sides. In a normally oriented front view, garment-left appears on screen-right; in the rear view it appears on screen-left. Preserve this through rotation. Never mirror the front to create the back.

## Layers and missing structure

Keep outerwear, lining, inner tops, belts and lower garments separate and correctly occluded. An inner top visible through an open jacket need not show through its back. Do not fuse a jacket and blouse into one panel.

Retain clear outfit accessories; show gloves and shoes as empty products without fake hands or feet. Do not add accessories to fill space or convert handheld props into trim.

Restore small occlusions by continuing fabric and seams. Use restrained same-fabric back panels, adding a closure only when needed for plausible wear. Do not fill intentional cutouts or open-back designs.

Extend one-piece garments continuously. Complete absent lower garments with compatible material, palette, period and formality, using uncomplicated detail. Do not force all outfits into skirts or trousers.

## Material behavior

Use wearing ease and cloth tension rather than skin-tight anatomy imprints. Cotton has soft folds; denim has firm structure; knitwear needs consistent stitch scale; silk needs gentle drape; leather needs believable stiffness. Sheer fabrics require layer-aware transparency. Reflective materials retain suitable highlights without becoming wet or metallic.

Empty collars and cuffs may show inside fabric, lining or background. No skin, translucent body or visible mannequin. Prevent extra sleeves, merged cuffs, broken straps and impossible seam intersections.
